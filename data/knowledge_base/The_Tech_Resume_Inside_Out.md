Chapter 1: Why Resumes and CVs are Important
Overview of Resume Review Process
The author, Gergely Orosz, shares his experience in
reviewing a vast number of resumes, noting that hiring
managers often make quick decisions based on first
impressions. Resumes that fail to effectively represent the
candidate can lead to missed opportunities.
The Goal of a Resume
The primary purpose of a resume is to secure an interview,
not to provide a comprehensive work history. It should be
crafted to immediately highlight the candidate's suitability
for a specific role.
Screening Process by Hiring Managers
Recruiters typically use a "yes/maybe/no" model to sift
through resumes quickly. The goal is to identify top
candidates who fit the position, emphasizing the need for
resumes to stand out positively.
Good vs. Great Resumes
- Good resumes accurately represent skills and experiences,
improving chances of passing initial screenings.
- Great resumes capture the attention of hiring managers.
They emphasize relevant experiences and demonstrate a clear
career progression, contrasting with "journeyman" resumes
that lack growth.
Case Study: Impact of Presentation
One discussed resume from an applicant who had worked at
prominent companies failed to effectively showcase their
capabilities. A revision focused on their significant
contributions transformed it into a standout application,
leading to more interviews.
Limitations of LinkedIn Profiles
Relying solely on LinkedIn profiles for applications is
inadequate. LinkedIn offers a generic view and lacks
customization for specific roles. A tailored resume is
essential for optimal impact.
Resume is Not Enough
A well-crafted resume is necessary but not sufficient to
guarantee interviews. Other external factors, such as position
availability, competition, and seniority requirements, also
play critical roles. Understanding these dynamics can help
candidates create resumes that resonate with hiring managers.
Conclusion
This chapter emphasizes the importance of crafting a resume
that accurately represents a candidate’s strengths and aligns
with the specific job requirements. While no single approach
guarantees success, a well-prepared resume can significantly
improve chances in a competitive job market.
Example
Key Point:Crafting a tailored resume is crucial for
job success.
Example:Imagine you're applying for a software
engineering role at a tech startup. Instead of listing
every job you've had, you choose to focus on your
relevant programming projects and how they directly
relate to the skills the company seeks. You highlight
your recent work on a machine learning model that
improved efficiency and your role in developing a
popular app. This careful selection of information and
presentation not only showcases your capabilities
effectively but also demonstrates to the hiring manager
that you understand their needs and how you fit into
their vision. In this way, your tailored resume becomes
your most powerful tool in landing that coveted
interview, setting you apart from the sea of generic
applications.
Critical Thinking
Key Point:The primary goal of a resume is to secure
an interview rather than offer a detailed career
history.
Critical Interpretation:While Gergely Orosz emphasizes
that the resume must effectively highlight a candidate’s
fit for the role to secure interviews, it's crucial to
consider that this may oversimplify the complexities of
hiring processes. Not all hiring managers use the same
criteria for evaluating candidates; some may value
comprehensive work histories or collaborative
experiences over concise highlights. The narrative
around resumes must also include the importance of soft
skills and interpersonal dynamics, which traditionally
do not appear on resumes but are often critical in
selection processes (Berry, 2018, 'What Hiring
Managers Want: The Role of Soft Skills'). Therefore,
while Orosz’s perspective on the resume's importance is
valuable, it should not be viewed as the only pathway to
success in job applications.

Chapter 2: The Hiring Pipeline
Overview of the Hiring Pipeline
The interview process, often perceived as unpredictable, can
be conceptualized as a hiring pipeline marked by significant
candidate drop-off at each stage. Understanding this
framework helps emphasize the importance of crafting a
strong resume.
People in the Recruitment Process
1.
Hiring Manager
: The key decision-maker who defines the role's
requirements, oversees the hiring process, and has the final
say on hiring.
2.
Recruiter
: Coordinates the recruitment process, ensuring candidates
move smoothly through stages while meeting the hiring
manager's criteria.
3.
Sourcer
: Actively seeks out and identifies potential candidates
through various channels, aiming to generate interest and
increase the candidate pool.
4.
Inbound Sourcer
: Handles incoming applications and prioritizes referrals.
5.
Recruitment Coordinator
: Manages logistical details and scheduling of interviews to
ensure smooth operations.
6.
Interview Panel
: A group of engineers that conducts technical interviews and
assesses candidates' fittingness for the role.
Understanding these roles helps candidates see the
recruitment process as a collaborative effort and not just a
series of individual screens.
Typical Stages of the Hiring Pipeline
1.
Applicant Tracking System (ATS) Queue
: Tracks application statuses; resumes may not be reviewed
depending on the volume of applications.
2.
Resume Screen
: Recruiters quickly assess resumes for fit; most candidates
drop off at this stage.
3.
Recruiter Screen
: Promising candidates undergo a phone interview focusing
on experience and motivation.
4.
Technical Screen
: Involves coding challenges and technical assessments by
engineers.
5.
Onsite Interview
: Multiple technical and soft-skill assessments, often
including coding and design challenges.
6.
Offer Stage
: Successful candidates receive offers, often from a pool of
numerous applicants.
Myths about ATS
Contrary to common misconceptions, ATS systems do not
automatically reject resumes. Humans control the application
process, and while ATS may assist in organizing information,
it is ultimately a recruiter or hiring manager who makes
rejection decisions.
Referrals and Networking
Referrals play a crucial role in the hiring pipeline,
significantly enhancing chances for candidates. Seeking
internal referrals can provide priority attention to resumes
and facilitate the recruiting process.
Resume Prioritization
Resumes are prioritized based on several factors:
- Referrals,
- Local candidates (no relocation),
- Candidates not needing visa sponsorship,
- Those requiring visas and relocation, which is generally
less preferred.
Competitive Nature of Hiring Pipelines
While large companies face intense competition among
applicants, smaller and mid-sized companies may have less
stringent hiring processes and can be more flexible regarding
qualifications and sponsorships.
Job Boards vs. Job Aggregators
Job aggregators, which compile postings from various
company career sites, can be more productive in finding
lesser-known job opportunities compared to traditional job
boards.
Summary and Actionable Insights
- Understand the roles within the hiring pipeline to tailor
your resume effectively.
- Utilize referrals to gain an advantage in the application
process.
- Explore job postings on aggregators for lesser-known
opportunities.
- Prioritize applications based on the likelihood of standing
out and the company's hiring policies.
Critical Thinking
Key Point:Understanding the roles in the hiring
pipeline greatly enhances your job application
strategy.
Critical Interpretation:The author posits that by
comprehending the roles of the hiring manager,
recruiters, and sourcers, candidates can tailor their
resumes more effectively to meet specific expectations.
However, one could argue that the emphasis on these
roles oversimplifies the complexities of recruitment, as
numerous external factors such as market demand, shifts
in company culture, and even personal biases may
significantly impact hiring outcomes. Literature in
organizational behavior, such as 'Hiring for Attitude' by
Mark Murphy, suggests that cultural fit often outweighs
the structured processes outlined, indicating that
candidates should not solely rely on understanding the
hiring pipeline but also on demonstrating their unique
contributions and adaptability.

Chapter 3: Resumes and COVID-19
Impact of COVID-19 on the Job Market
The COVID-19 pandemic has drastically altered the tech job
market, creating a highly competitive environment for
software developers seeking employment. By September
2020, over 80,000 tech workers had been laid off, resulting in
fewer hiring opportunities and a reversal from a developer's
market to an employer's market. As remote work becomes
commonplace, the pool of applicants has expanded, leading
to increased competition for job openings.
Challenges for Job Seekers
With a large number of applications for each available
position, candidates must not only possess strong resumes
but also emphasize their skills and experiences to stand out
among a sea of applicants. New graduates and interns face
even more significant challenges, as many companies have
rescinded offers and are wary of hiring inexperienced
candidates.
Hiring Trends and Insights
Hiring managers report an overwhelming spike in
applications, with some experiencing a 20-30x increase in the
number of candidates applying for roles. This trend
highlights the necessity for tailored resumes and the
importance of referrals in securing interviews.

Chapter 4 Summary : 4. Tech Resume
Basics
Chapter 4 Tech Resume Basics
This chapter emphasizes the unwritten rules of resume
writing for tech positions and what recruiters look for during
the first scan. Key elements include clarity, simplicity, and
professionalism.
The First Glance
Recruiters typically seek out the following key pieces of
information at first glance:
-
Years of Experience
: Clearly indicate how long you’ve been working, allowing
recruiters to assess your level relative to position
expectations.
-
Relevant Technologies
: List technologies pertinent to the job description to ease the
recruiter’s search for relevant experience.
-
Work Experience
: Highlight measurable accomplishments and consistent
impact in previous roles. Quantify where possible.
-
Work Authorization and Visa Status
: If applicable, clarify your authorization to work to avoid
being placed in a lower priority/potential rejection category.
-
Anything that Stands Out
: Notable achievements or affiliations can make your resume
memorable.
Levels at Different Companies
Job postings often correspond to internal level designs,
which may not always be disclosed in the listings.
Understanding these mappings is crucial, as they help gauge
career progression across companies, although they are not
exact.
Ground Rules
A professional resume should adhere to basic guidelines:
1.
Good Grammar and No Typos
: Use tools like Grammarly to avoid errors that can harm
your credibility.
2.
Basic Contact Details
: Include only essential information, omitting irrelevant
details like full mailing addresses.
3.
Dates in Reverse Chronological Order
: Present work and education experiences starting with the
most recent.
4.
Avoid Personal Information
: Exclude details that could introduce bias, such as photos,
birth dates, and other personal identifiers.
5.
Two Pages or Less
: Ideally, keep to one page, especially for those with limited
experience.
Simplicity and Consistency
To attract attention, ensure your resume is easy to read:
-
Formatting
: Maintain consistent styles throughout.
-
Bullet Points
: Use bullet points instead of paragraphs for improved
readability, avoiding sub-bullets.
-
Dates
: Present dates clearly and consistently.
-
Use PDF Format
: Save and send your resume as a PDF to maintain
formatting.
Avoiding Biases: Personal Details and Photos
Personal details on resumes can lead to unconscious bias
during recruitment. Photos, ages, and personal situations
might skew perception. In tech hiring, focus should remain
on skills and qualifications:
- Do not include unnecessary personal details that may
trigger biases.
- Research local norms regarding resume submissions, as
practices can vary by region.
Recap: Actions to Improve Your Resume
To enhance your resume:
- Choose a simple, consistent template.
- Remove all unnecessary personal information.
- List relevant technologies and experiences prominently on
the first page.
- Ensure dates are easy to scan.
- Use bullet points for clarity.
- Conduct thorough spell and grammar checks.
- Save and name the final document appropriately as a PDF.
By adhering to these principles, you can create a professional
and compelling resume that stands out to recruiters in the
tech industry.
Example
Key Point:Simplicity and Clarity
Example:When crafting your tech resume, visualize
how a recruiter sifts through countless applications;
ensure your experience and skills are effortlessly
recognizable. Use bullet points to list relevant
technologies and achievements so they leap out at first
glance. For instance, if you’ve used Python, JavaScript,
and AWS in projects, place these prominently right at
the beginning. This clarity not only showcases your
suitability for the role, but also makes your resume
easier for recruiters to navigate, ultimately enhancing
your chances of landing an interview.
Chapter 5 Summary : 5. Resume
Structure
Chapter 5 Resume Structure
There are two types of software developers: those who have
secured their first full-time job and those who are seeking
one. Recruiters focus on different details depending on your
experience level. For new or aspiring developers, internships,
education, projects, and achievements matter most, while the
work experience and professional skills take precedence once
employed. How you structure your resume should reflect
your experience stage.
Structure for Interns, New Grads, and Bootcamp
Grads
For candidates with limited experience, emphasize the
following in order of priority:
1.
Real-World Work Experience
: List any software development roles and highlight
accomplishments.
2.
Internships
: Detail your impact and contributions, as these can set you
apart.
3.
Open Source Contributions
: List significant projects you have been involved with.
4.
Education
: Include school rankings and grades if they are notable.
5.
Impactful Projects
: Mention prominent projects and their significance.
6.
Leadership and Tutoring Roles
: Include any relevant extracurricular activities.
New Grad Resume Structure
A suggested structure for new grads includes:
-
Resume Length
: Keep it to one page.
-
Work Experience and Internships
: Start with these sections.
-
Projects
: Highlight your contributions and link to the source.
-
Education
: Provide expected graduation dates and notable
achievements.
-
Languages and Technologies
: List relevant skills upfront.
-
Interests
: Briefly mention personal interests to facilitate conversation.
Key Recruitment Insights
Tailor your resume to the job description to stand out.
Highlight relevant experiences and extracurricular activities.
Understand possible internship timing and clarify
availability. Thorough interview preparation is
crucial—research the company and interviewers to
demonstrate genuine interest.
Structure with Work Experience
When you have more professional experience:
1.
Work Experience
: Place it at the top, showcasing your current title and key
projects.
2.
Languages and Technologies Section
: Include relevant technologies and skills.
3.
Education
: Reduce details as you gain more experience.
4.
Extracurricular Activities
: List patents, publications, or notable projects.
5.
Certifications
: Only include relevant certifications.
6.
Projects
: Focus on standout projects and their significance.
What Recruiters Look For
Recruiters seek alignment with job requirements, relevant
experiences, and adaptability. Tailor your resume for each
position to reflect your fit.
Languages and Technologies
Present these clearly:
1.
Separate Section
: List relevant technologies you are proficient in.
2.
Experience Integration
: Mention languages and technologies within your job
descriptions.
3.
Differentiation
: Distinguish between hands-on and less familiar languages if
necessary.
Telling a Story
Ensure your resume conveys a clear progression of your
career:
- Highlight promotions and roles accurately.
- Use readable date formats.
- Avoid irrelevant job listings that do not support your
narrative.
Ethical Title Adjustments
Consider adjusting your job title only when your actual
responsibilities differ significantly from the title.
Summary Section
- Many resumes include a summary that may not be
thoroughly read by recruiters.
- For less experienced candidates, a tailored summary may
enhance your resume’s appeal, while for seasoned
professionals, a concise, effective summary can illustrate
standout experiences.
Structure for Senior and Above
Experienced candidates:
1.
Major Accomplishments
: Include notable deliverables and don't strictly adhere to the
two-page limit.
2.
Separate Resumes for Roles
: Tailor distinct resumes for management versus individual
contributor positions.
Recap: Actions to Improve Your Resume
To enhance your resume, ensure sections reflect priority
based on your experience, highlight relevant technologies,
tell a coherent story, and tailor the summary. Adjust detail
levels according to how recently you've held positions and
maintain clarity on dates. Ensure the overall length aligns
with your level of experience, avoiding excessive length
unless warranted by accomplishments.
Example
Key Point:Tailor your resume to fit your experience
level and the job you're applying for.
Example:Imagine you're a recent bootcamp graduate
looking for your first software developer job. Your
resume needs to highlight your impressive coding
projects and any internships you've completed, ensuring
you present your skills in a way that speaks directly to
what the recruiters are seeking. If you adapt your
resume to showcase these experiences at the top, along
with a clear explanation of your technologies, you stand
a much better chance of grabbing the attention of hiring
managers who sift through countless applications. This
tailored approach not only demonstrates your
qualifications but also reflects your understanding of the
tech industry’s requirements.
Chapter 6 Summary : 6. Standing Out
Chapter 6: Standing Out
Your resume competes among many applications, so it’s
crucial to make it memorable. A generic resume fails to
capture attention and lacks specific, impactful content. This
chapter focuses on how to differentiate your resume by
emphasizing results, using quantifiable metrics, tailoring it to
job postings, and understanding the different requirements of
various companies.
Results, Impact, and Your Contribution
-
Focus on Achievements:
Emphasize what you achieved rather than just listing duties.
Use a structured framework to express the impact of your
work.
-
Quantify Results:
Incorporate numbers to articulate your impact, which is
often lacking in many resumes. For instance, instead of
saying, “built a tool,” say, “led a team of 3 developers to
build a framework adopted by 15 teams and 50+ developers.”
-
Use Active Language:
Select active verbs (e.g., "led," "managed") over passive
constructions. While technologies used are important,
prioritize conveying your impact first.
How to Craft Compelling Bullet Points
- Start compiling bullet points for your resume early; note
everything you can recall about your work.
- Don’t fixate on buzzwords or action words. Instead,
concentrate on specifics and detail, ideally backed by
quantifiable results.

Chapter 7 Summary : 7. Common
Mistakes
Chapter 7: Common Mistakes
Poor Format
-
Hard-to-scan resumes
: Avoid multi-column formats; opt for simple, one-column
designs for better readability.
-
Too much bolding
: Limit bolding to essential elements such as dates, titles, and
companies to maintain clarity.
-
Overly flashy designs
: Focus on content over style; avoid distracting templates that
hinder information retrieval.
-
Inconsistent formatting
: Use uniform font sizes and properly align content to
demonstrate attention to detail.
-
Sloppy language
: Utilize clear, professional language; avoid informal phrases
like "etc." or "and so on."
Forgetting About Your Audience
-
Internal jargon
: Avoid using company-specific acronyms or project names
that may confuse external readers.
-
Lack of tailoring
: Customize your resume for each application, highlighting
relevant experience first.
-
Clichés over evidence
: Replace vague statements with specific contributions and
results to showcase your abilities.
-
Verbosity
: Keep sentences concise to cater to the limited attention span
of recruiters.
Unnecessary Details
-
Photos
: It’s recommended to omit photos due to potential biases.
-
Excessive contact information
: Limit details to four key contact points such as phone,
email, and LinkedIn.
-
Spoken languages
: Only include relevant languages if applicable to the position
you're applying for.
-
Self-assessments
: Avoid subjective ratings of your skills; instead, simply list
your proficiencies.
-
References
: Omit references or statements about their availability to
save space.
-
Quotes praising you
: Your resume should feature your achievements rather than
comments from others.
Links
-
Outdated or non-functional links
: Ensure all links are current and direct to professional
content.
-
Full URLs
: Use descriptive phrases instead of complete URLs to keep
the resume clean.
-
Link visibility
: Make links blend in with the overall design to avoid
distracting readers.
Recap: Actions to Improve Your Resume
1. Remove any photos.
2. Limit contact details to a maximum of four.
3. Verify that all links work and are relevant.
4. Ensure the resume is easy to scan; get feedback from
peers.
5. Keep formatting simple and consistent.
6. Eliminate internal jargon.
7. Avoid including languages and references sections.
8. Standardize link formatting so they do not attract undue
attention.
Critical Thinking
Key Point:Importance of Tailoring Resumes for
Different Audiences
Critical Interpretation:One critical point in this chapter
emphasizes the necessity of tailoring your resume to
each specific job application, ensuring that relevant
experiences are prominently displayed. While Gergely
Orosz advocates for this approach as a means to
effectively engage recruiters, it's crucial for readers to
consider that the ease of tailoring resumes can vary
based on individual circumstances and industry
standards. Not every candidate will have the luxury of
extensive experience in multiple areas that can be
highlighted for diverse applications, and generic
templates might still serve their purpose. Critics of this
tailored approach might point to sources such as 'The
Resume Handbook' by Richard N. Bolles, suggesting
that a well-crafted, general resume can also be valuable,
as it allows for a broader audience reach without the
need for frequent rewrites.
Chapter 8 Summary : 8. Different
Experience Levels, Different Career

Chapter 8: Different Experience Levels, Different
Career Paths
Current College and University Students
- Focus on building a strong resume through internships,
extracurricular activities, and publishing projects.
- Internships provide valuable real-world experience and
should be sought out early.
- Volunteering for campus activities aids in developing
leadership and mentorship skills.
- Publishing class projects, especially final ones, enhances
your portfolio and showcases your skills.
Bootcamp Graduates
- Distinguish yourself through projects, as bootcamp
experience alone may not suffice.
- Strategies to enhance your profile include:
1. Publishing well-documented projects on GitHub with
clear READMEs and tests.
2. Contributing to popular open-source projects to gain
practical experience.
3. Transforming take-home coding challenges into polished
projects to showcase capabilities.
Career Changers
- Highlight your motivation for transitioning and list relevant
skills and projects at the top of your resume.
- Include a brief summary of past work experiences with
transferable skills and keep the resume concise.
- Aim for a one-page resume focused on standout projects
and software development skills.
Career Breaks
- Older breaks typically do not require explanation; focus on
recent breaks if relevant.
- Present any break as a narrative—as an opportunity for
personal growth or skill development.
- Examples of career breaks include travel for cultural
experiences, pursuing personal interests, or caregiving.
Senior Engineers and Above
- Emphasize results and impact in your resume, focusing on
collaboration and influence.
- Tailor the summary section to match job requirements and
move education to the second page to reduce age bias.
- Highlight relevant technologies and reduce detail in early
work experiences.
Tech Leads
- Demonstrate your ability to enhance team performance and
resolve delivery issues.
- Showcase specific outcomes and the context of your
leadership experiences.
- Create distinct resumes for tech lead and engineering
manager applications.
Engineering Managers
- Tell a compelling story on your resume about team
improvements and project impacts.
- Align your experiences with the specific values and needs
of the company.
- Focus on team-oriented achievements and consider
reaching out to hiring managers before applying.
Final Thoughts
- Engineering resumes should reflect your unique journey
and capabilities.
- Leverage resources like LinkedIn for inspiration and seek
feedback from peers in the industry for continuous
improvement.

Exercises to Polish Your Resume
Write Two Different Resumes
To enhance your resume, create two distinct versions using
different templates. Avoid repeating achievements from the

8.Question
How can someone improve their resume if they feel their
experience is not traditional?
Answer:If your experience is non-traditional, consider
emphasizing your transferable skills, relevant projects, and
any self-taught experiences or bootcamp training, particularly
if applying to companies that value diverse backgrounds.
9.Question
What should you do if the job description encourages
candidates from non-tech industries to apply?
Answer:Reflect on any relevant experiences from your

consider including a brief summary section that highlights
how those experiences relate to the tech role you're applying
for.

Why might a resume with unnecessary personal details,

photo can introduce bias and distract from your professional
qualifications, making your resume less appealing to
recruiters who are looking for concise, relevant information.

Chapter 14 | 14. Advice for Hiring Managers on
Running a Good Screening Process| Q&A
1.Question
Why is it important for hiring managers to be involved in
the resume screening process?
Answer:Hiring managers need to understand how
resumes are filtered so they do not miss out on
promising candidates who may not present
themselves well on paper. Their involvement ensures
that potentially great hires aren’t eliminated
prematurely.
2.Question
What are the risks of relying solely on resume screening?
Answer:Resume screening can lead to the rejection of
talented individuals who may have poorly formatted resumes
but could excel in the role. This is because resumes often
provide a weak indicator of true candidate ability.
3.Question
How can hiring managers mitigate unconscious biases in
the recruitment process?
Answer:By being aware of their biases and incorporating
diverse criteria and structures in their job descriptions and
hiring evaluations, hiring managers can broaden their
candidate pool and improve diversity.
4.Question
What steps can hiring managers take to improve their
recruitment process?
Answer:They can involve other team members in screening
resumes to calibrate expectations, allocate dedicated time for
hiring tasks, create structured feedback loops, and regularly
assess candidate progress and diversity metrics.
5.Question
Why is it important to write a well-thought-out job
description?
Answer:A clear and specific job description helps attract the
right candidates and avoids miscommunication about the
skills and experiences necessary for the role. It also ensures
that the recruitment process is fair and transparent.
6.Question
What should hiring managers do to foster a strong
relationship with their recruitment team?
Answer:They should communicate openly about their needs,
be involved in the recruitment process, provide feedback, and
recognize the recruiters as partners in the hiring strategy.
7.Question
Why might it be beneficial to use assessment-based
screening methods over traditional resume screening?
Answer:Assessment-based methods can uncover skills and
potential that aren't evident in resumes, allowing hiring
managers to find 'hidden gems' who might excel if given a
chance, despite a less-than-perfect resume.
8.Question
How can hiring managers ensure their hiring processes
lead to diverse teams?
Answer:They should monitor their hiring pipelines for
diversity metrics, aim to reduce biases in job descriptions and
requirements, and purposefully seek out candidates from
various backgrounds.
9.Question
What example illustrates the importance of hiring outside
traditional criteria?
Answer:One hiring manager’s decision to consider a
candidate who was a semi-professional musician led to hiring
a great engineer who wouldn’t have been seen through a
traditional resume lens.
10.Question
How should feedback be incorporated into the hiring
process?
Answer:Establish a clear feedback policy for candidates at
different stages, ensuring that candidates receive constructive
and timely feedback to improve their future applications.
